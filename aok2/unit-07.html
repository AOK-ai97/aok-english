Rule · Qoida
Past Continuous — o'tmishda davom etayotgan ish
I / he / she / it was + V-ingAt 8 pm I was watching the fireworks.
you / we / they were + V-ingThey were dancing all night.
− / ?She wasn't singing. Were you cooking sumalak?
when / while
uzun ish (PC) + when + qisqa ish (PS)We were eating plov when the music started.
while + PC, … PSWhile I was dancing, I lost my phone.
ikkita parallel uzun ishWhile Mum was cooking, Dad was decorating the house.

Reading · O'qish · Reading Part 5 format
Read the text. Complete the summary (1–4) with ONE word from the text. Then choose A, B, C or D (5–6). — xulosa (BITTA so'z) + A–D
A Night of Sumalak

Every March, before Navruz, women in many Uzbek neighbourhoods gather to cook sumalak, a sweet dish made from young wheat. Cooking it is not quick: the wheat is grown for several days, crushed, and then cooked in a huge pot for almost twenty-four hours. Someone has to stir the pot all the time, so the women take turns through the night.

Last year I joined my neighbours for the first time. When I arrived at about ten in the evening, the fire was already burning and the older women were telling stories. While some were stirring the pot, others were singing traditional songs. Nobody was looking at their phone — everyone was talking and laughing.

There is an old custom: while the sumalak is cooking, people make a wish. Small stones are put at the bottom of the pot. If you find one in your bowl, it is said that your wish will come true. I didn't find a stone, but my little cousin did, and she was so happy that she showed it to everyone.

At sunrise the sumalak was finally ready. It was dark brown and tasted like sweet chocolate, even though no sugar is added. We shared it with neighbours, friends and even strangers who were walking past. For me, that night was not only about food, but also about being part of a community.

Summary
Sumalak is cooked for about a day, so women (1) ______ the pot all night. During the evening, people sing songs and tell (2) ______. According to an old (3) ______, finding a stone means your wish will come true. Although no (4) ______ is used, the dish tastes very sweet.
5.What surprised the writer most about the evening?
A) the size of the pot
B) that nobody used their phones
C) the cold weather
D) the number of men
6.What is the main idea of the last paragraph?
A) Sumalak is like chocolate.
B) The writer found a stone.
C) The night was about people, not only food.

A Wedding to Remember

Last summer I went to my cousin's wedding in Fergana. Over five hundred guests came. While the guests were arriving, musicians were playing the drums and the karnay. The bride was wearing a beautiful white dress. At about nine o'clock, while everyone was dancing, the lights suddenly went out! For a few minutes, nobody could see anything. But the musicians didn't stop. They were still playing when the lights came back on. Everyone laughed, and the party continued until midnight.

1.The wedding was in Fergana.
2.About fifty guests came.
3.Musicians played while the guests were arriving.
4.The lights went out while people were eating.
5.The musicians stopped playing in the dark.
