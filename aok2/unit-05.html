Rule · Qoida
Passive — ish kim tomonidan emas, ishning o'zi muhim
Present: am / is / are + V3Paper is recycled in this factory.
Millions of trees are cut down every year.
Past: was / were + V3This park was opened in 2019.
The bottles were collected by students.
− / ?Glass isn't recycled here. Was the forest destroyed?
by — kim tomonidanThe poster was made by our class.

Active: Students clean the river. → Passive: The river is cleaned (by students).

Reading · O'qish · Reading Part 3 format
Read the text. Choose the correct heading for each paragraph (1–5) from the list (A–G). There are two extra headings. — sarlavhalar (2 ta ortiqcha)
The Plastic Problem

1. Every year, about 300 million tonnes of plastic are produced in the world. Only a small part of it is recycled. The rest is burnt, buried or simply thrown away, and much of it ends up in rivers and seas.

2. Plastic doesn't disappear. A plastic bottle can stay in nature for 450 years. Over time it breaks into very small pieces called microplastics. These tiny pieces have been found in fish, in salt and even in drinking water.

3. Some countries have already taken action. In Rwanda, plastic bags were banned in 2008, and today the streets are some of the cleanest in Africa. In many European supermarkets, customers now pay for every plastic bag, so they bring their own bags from home.

4. Young people are often the most active. In Tashkent, a group of school students started collecting plastic bottles in their neighbourhood. The bottles are sold to a recycling company, and the money is used to buy books for the school library.

5. Everyone can help in small ways: carry a cloth bag, use a metal water bottle, and say no to plastic straws. If millions of people make these small changes, the result will be huge.

Reading · O'qish
F Read and write T (True) or F (False). — o'qing: to'g'ri — T, noto'g'ri — F
The Aral Sea

The Aral Sea was once the fourth largest lake in the world. In the 1960s, the water from two big rivers was used for cotton fields. Year by year the sea became smaller. Now a large part of it is a desert. Many fish died, and the local people lost their jobs. Today, millions of trees are being planted on the dry seabed to stop the sand and salt storms. A small part of the sea in the north has been saved, and fish have returned there.

1.The Aral Sea was once very big.
2.River water was used for cotton.
3.The sea is bigger now.
4.Trees are planted on the dry seabed.
5.No fish live in the Aral Sea now.
