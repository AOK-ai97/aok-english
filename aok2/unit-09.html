Rule · Qoida
Qarama-qarshilik bog'lovchilari
although + gapAlthough the advert was short, it was very effective.
whereas — taqqoslashTV is popular with adults, whereas teenagers prefer YouTube.
However, — yangi gap boshidaInfluencers earn a lot. However, their job is not easy.
despite + ot / V-ingDespite the rain, we went. · Despite being young, he has a big channel.

⚠️ although dan keyin to'liq gap; despite dan keyin ot yoki V-ing: despite it was raining → despite the rain.

Reading · O'qish · Reading Part 3 format
Choose the correct heading for each paragraph (1–5) from A–G. There are two extra headings. — sarlavhalar (2 ta ortiqcha)
The World of Advertising

1. A hundred years ago, adverts were simple: a picture and a few words in a newspaper. Today companies spend billions of dollars on advertising, and the average person sees thousands of adverts every day — on TV, on buses, on phones and even in video games.

2. Many companies no longer pay only for TV adverts. Instead, they pay influencers to show their products. Although followers know this is advertising, they often trust a blogger more than a TV advert, because the blogger seems like a friend.

3. Some adverts are designed especially for children. Bright colours, cartoon characters and free toys make children want the product. In several countries, adverts for sweets and fast food are not allowed during children's TV programmes.

4. Not all adverts are bad. Social adverts teach people to wear seat belts, save water or stop smoking. A clever social advert can change the behaviour of millions of people in a short time.

5. So how can we protect ourselves? Experts suggest asking three questions: Who made this advert? What do they want me to do? What information is missing? Despite being simple, these questions can save us a lot of money.

Headings
A) Adverts that help society
B) How advertising has grown
C) Why we believe bloggers
D) The most expensive adverts in history
E) Think before you buy
F) Rules to protect the youngest
G) How to become an influencer

Reading · O'qish
F Read and write T (True) or F (False). — o'qing: to'g'ri — T, noto'g'ri — F
Can You Believe Everything Online?

Last month a video appeared on social media. It showed a 'shark' swimming in a street in Tashkent after heavy rain. Although it was fake news, more than 100,000 people shared it in one day. Some people were really scared. However, a journalist decided to fact-check the video. She found that the shark was from a film made in 2015. Despite this, many people still believed the video was real. The lesson is simple: before you share a headline, check where it comes from.

1.The video showed a shark in Tashkent.
2.The video was real.
3.A journalist checked the video.
4.The shark was from a film.
5.Nobody believed the video after the journalist's report.
