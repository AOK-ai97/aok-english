Rule · Qoida
Passive — takrorlash
PresentVitamins are found in fruit.
PastThe first vaccine was invented in 1796.
can / should + be + V3Junk food should be avoided. Stress can be reduced by sport.
It is + V3 + that… — umumiy fikr (B1+)
It is said that…It is said that green tea is good for the heart.
It is widely believed that…It is widely believed that sport reduces stress.

Reading · O'qish · Reading Part 4 format
Read the text. Questions 1–4: choose A, B, C or D. Questions 5–9: True, False or No Information. — 1–4: A–D; 5–9: True / False / No Information
The Village Where People Live Longer

On the Japanese island of Okinawa, there are more people over one hundred years old than almost anywhere else in the world. Scientists have studied the islanders for more than forty years to understand their secret. Their answer is surprisingly simple: the way people eat, move and live together.

Okinawans eat a lot of vegetables, especially sweet potatoes, and very little meat or sugar. Their meals are small. In fact, they follow an old rule called hara hachi bu, which means "eat until you are eighty per cent full". It is said that this habit alone helps them avoid many illnesses.

Old people on Okinawa don't go to the gym, but they are very active. Most of them work in their gardens every day, walk to the market and sit on the floor instead of chairs, so they get up and down many times a day. This keeps their legs and backs strong.

Perhaps the most important thing is friendship. Many Okinawans belong to a small group of friends called a moai. The friends meet regularly, help each other and share their problems. It is widely believed that strong relationships reduce stress and help people live longer.

However, things are changing. Young Okinawans now eat more fast food, and many of them have moved to big cities. Doctors are worried that the island may soon lose its famous long life.

1.Why have scientists studied Okinawa?
2.What does "hara hachi bu" mean?
3.Why do old people have strong legs?
4.What is a "moai"?
5.Okinawans eat a lot of sugar.
6.Older Okinawans often sit on the floor.
7.Moai groups meet every Sunday.
8.Young Okinawans eat differently from older people.
9.Okinawa has the best hospitals in Japan.

Small Changes, Big Results

A year ago, Botir was always tired and stressed. He ate junk food, drank fizzy drinks and slept only five hours a night. Then his doctor gave him some advice. Now Botir walks to work every day and works out twice a week. He has stopped drinking fizzy drinks and eats more vegetables. It is said that small changes can change your life, and Botir agrees. He has lost eight kilos and feels much happier. 'My mental health is better too,' he says.
