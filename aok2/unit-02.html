Rule · Qoida
Present Perfect yoki Past Simple?
Present Perfect — vaqt aytilmagan / natija hozir muhimI've lost my phone. (hozir telefonim yo'q)
She's downloaded the app.
Past Simple — aniq o'tgan vaqtI lost my phone yesterday.
She downloaded it two days ago.
Past Simple so'zlariyesterday, last week, in 2020, two hours ago, When…?
Present Perfect so'zlariever, never, just, already, yet, so far, this week
just / already / yet
just — hozirginaI've just sent the email.
already — allaqachonHe's already updated the app.
yet — hali (− / ? oxirida)I haven't finished yet. Have you charged your phone yet?

Savol PP bilan, tafsilot PS bilan: Have you ever broken a phone? — Yes. I broke it last summer.

Reading · O'qish · Reading Part 4 format
Read the text. For questions 1–4, choose A, B, C or D. For questions 5–9, write True, False or No Information. — 1–4: A–D tanlang; 5–9: True / False / No Information
The Boy Who Fixes Phones

When Sanjar was eleven, his father's old phone stopped working. Instead of throwing it away, Sanjar opened it with a small screwdriver. "I didn't know anything about phones," he remembers. "I just wanted to see what was inside." He watched videos online for weeks and finally found the problem: a broken battery. He bought a new one for 40,000 som, and the phone worked again.

That was four years ago. Since then, Sanjar has repaired more than two hundred phones. At first he fixed phones for his family and neighbours for free. Then people started to pay him. Now he has a small table in his uncle's shop at the market, and he works there every Saturday and Sunday.

Sanjar's parents were worried at the beginning. "We thought it was a waste of time," says his mother. "But his school marks have not got worse. In fact, his maths is better now." Sanjar has saved most of the money he has earned. He wants to study computer engineering at university.

Not everything has been easy. Last year he broke an expensive phone screen and had to pay for a new one himself. "It was a hard lesson," he says. "Now I always tell customers about the risks before I start." His advice for other teenagers? "Don't be afraid to try. You learn the most from your mistakes."

1.How did Sanjar first learn to repair phones?
2.What was wrong with his father's phone?
3.Where does Sanjar work now?
4.What happened last year?
5.Sanjar was eleven when he opened his first phone.
6.He has always asked people to pay for his work.
7.His parents liked his hobby from the start.
8.Sanjar's maths has improved.
9.Sanjar's uncle also repairs phones.

Grandma's First Smartphone

My grandmother is seventy, and last month she got her first smartphone. At first she was afraid of it. She asked me, 'What if I break it?' I have already taught her how to make video calls and send messages. She has just learnt how to take photos, and she has already sent me about a hundred! She hasn't learnt how to use the internet yet, but she wants to read the news online. Yesterday she forgot her password and called me three times. But I'm proud of her. She has learnt a lot in one month.

1.Grandma got her smartphone last year.
2.She was afraid of the phone at first.
3.She has learnt to make video calls.
4.She already reads the news online.
5.Yesterday she forgot her password.
