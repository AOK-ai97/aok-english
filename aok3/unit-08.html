Rule · Qoida
Passive — barcha zamonlar
ZamonShaklMisol
Present Simpleis / are + V3Millions of photos are uploaded every day.
Present Continuousis / are being + V3New rules are being written.
Present Perfecthas / have been + V3The video has been viewed 2 million times.
Past Simple / Continuouswas / were (being) + V3The account was hacked. · It was being watched.
Future / Modalwill / must / can be + V3Fake news must be removed.
have something done — boshqaga qildirmoq
have + narsa + V3I had my phone repaired. (o'zim emas, usta tuzatdi)
get + narsa + V3 (og'zaki)She got her account verified.

A Week Without Notifications

Last month our class did an experiment: for seven days, we switched off all notifications on our phones. We could still use social media, but only when we decided to open the apps — not when the phone told us to. At first it felt strange. On the first day, I checked my phone more than fifty times, just in case I had missed something. By the third day, however, I noticed that I was checking it much (1) . My concentration in lessons improved, I felt less stressed, and I finished my homework faster. Some students found the experiment harder. My friend Aziz said he felt anxious, because he was afraid of missing important messages from his (2) group. Interestingly, at the end of the week, nobody had missed anything important. Our teacher explained that for some people social media is almost an addiction, and that apps are designed to keep us on our (3) as long as possible, and notifications are one of their most powerful tools. After the experiment, most of us decided to keep our (4) switched off for at least some apps. I still use social media every day, but now it feels like a choice rather than an (5) . I think every class should try this (6) .

Reading · O'qish
F Read and write T (True), F (False) or NG (Not Given). — T — to'g'ri, F — noto'g'ri, NG — matnda aytilmagan
Life After Deleting Instagram

At twenty, Kamola had 15,000 followers and spent about five hours a day on social media. Her posts were seen by thousands of people, but she felt lonely. 'I compared myself with everyone. Every photo had to be edited and filtered,' she says. In 2023 she deleted her accounts for a month as an experiment. At first she felt anxious and kept reaching for her phone. After two weeks, she was sleeping better and had started reading again. She has since returned to social media, but only for thirty minutes a day. 'I still post, but I don't check how many likes I've received.'

1.Kamola spent five hours a day on social media.
2.She felt lonely despite her followers.
3.She deleted her accounts for a year.
4.She was sleeping better after two weeks.
5.She has never used social media again.
