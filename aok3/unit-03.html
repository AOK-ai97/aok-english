Rule · Qoida
Third Conditional — o'tmishdagi xayoliy shart (afsus)
If + had + V3, would have + V3If I had known about the traffic, I would have taken the metro.
−If we hadn't moved to the city, I wouldn't have found this job.
?What would you have done if you had missed the bus?
could have / might haveIf she had studied, she could have passed.

Mixed (bonus): If I had grown up in a village, I would be calmer now. (o'tmish → hozir)

Reading · O'qish · Reading Part 3 format
Read the text. Choose the correct heading for each paragraph (1–6) from A–H. There are two extra headings. — 6 abzats, 8 sarlavha (2 ta ortiqcha)
City or Village?

1. For the first time in history, more people live in cities than in the countryside. By 2050, the United Nations predicts, almost seven out of ten people on Earth will be city dwellers. In Uzbekistan too, young people from rural areas move to Tashkent every year in search of work and education.

2. The reasons are easy to understand. Cities offer universities, hospitals, cinemas and, most importantly, jobs. Salaries are usually higher, and there are more opportunities to build a career. If my cousin hadn't moved to the capital, he would never have found work as a programmer.

3. But city life has a price. Rent can take half of a young worker's salary, and many people spend two hours a day commuting. Traffic jams, noise and poor air quality are part of everyday life, and doctors link them to stress and health problems.

4. Villages, meanwhile, face the opposite problem. When young people leave, schools close, shops disappear and only the elderly stay behind. Some villages in Europe have so few residents that they offer free houses to anyone who agrees to live there.

5. Interestingly, technology may change this trend. With fast internet, many people can now work from home. After 2020, thousands of workers left big cities for smaller towns, where houses are cheaper and life is calmer, while keeping their city jobs.

6. Perhaps the answer is not choosing one or the other but making both better. If villages had good roads, internet and schools, fewer people would need to leave. And if cities had more parks and better public transport, living there would be far more pleasant.

Back to the Village

Ten years ago, Jasur moved from his village near Namangan to Tashkent. He found a job in an office, but his life was stressful. He spent two hours a day in traffic jams, and most of his salary went on rent. 'If I had known how hard it would be, I would have stayed at home,' he admits. Last year, Jasur decided to go back. With his savings he opened a small farm and an online shop for dried fruit. Now he sells to customers all over the country. The air quality is better, his commute is five minutes, and his children play outside every day. 'The village has changed too,' he says. 'There is fast internet now, and that changed everything.'

1.Jasur moved to Tashkent ten years ago.
2.He spent a lot of money on rent.
3.He regrets moving back to the village.
4.He sells dried fruit online.
5.His wife also works on the farm.
