Rule · Qoida
wish / if only — orzu va afsus
hozirgi holat → wish + Past SimpleI wish people used less plastic. (lekin ishlatishadi)
o'tmish → wish + Past PerfectI wish we had protected the Aral Sea. (himoya qilmadik)
boshqalarning harakati → wish + wouldI wish my neighbours would stop burning rubbish.
If only — kuchliroqIf only it would rain! · If only we had acted earlier!
It's (high) time + Past Simple
It's time we + V2It's time we changed our habits.
It's high time + V2 (kuchli)It's high time governments took action.

⚠️ Shakl o'tgan zamon, lekin ma'no — hozir / kelajak.

Reading · O'qish · Reading Part 5 format
Read the text. Then complete the summary and answer the questions on the next page. — matn — savollar keyingi sahifada
The Sea That Disappeared

Sixty years ago, the Aral Sea was the fourth-largest lake in the world. Fishing boats worked on its waters, and the town of Muynak was a busy port with a large fish factory. Today, ships lie rusting in the sand, and the nearest water is more than a hundred kilometres away.

The disaster began in the 1960s, when Soviet planners decided to use the water of the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers to grow cotton in the desert. Huge canals were built, and by the 1980s very little water reached the sea. It started to shrink rapidly. By 2010 it had lost about ninety per cent of its water.

The consequences have been terrible. As the sea dried, the climate of the region became more extreme: summers are hotter, winters colder, and rain is rarer. Strong winds now carry salt and dust from the dry sea bed across hundreds of kilometres, damaging crops and people's health. Thousands of fishermen lost their jobs.

Yet there is some hope. In the north, Kazakhstan built a dam in 2005, and the small northern part of the sea has started to recover: the water level has risen and fish have returned. In Uzbekistan, a huge project is planting desert plants, such as saxaul, on the dry sea bed. Their roots hold the sand and stop the salt storms. More than 1.7 million hectares have already been planted.

The story of the Aral Sea is often called one of the worst environmental disasters of the 20th century. Many local people say, "If only we had understood the consequences earlier." It is high time the world learnt from this lesson.

The Melting Ice of the Pamirs

The glaciers of the Pamir mountains supply water to millions of people in Central Asia. However, scientists say that they have lost about a third of their ice in the last fifty years. As the ice melts, rivers will first carry more water, which can cause floods. But in the long term there will be less water for agriculture, and droughts will become more common. 'I wish people understood that this is not a problem for the future — it is happening now,' says one researcher. It is high time countries in the region worked together to use water more carefully.
