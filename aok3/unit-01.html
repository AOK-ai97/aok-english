Reading · O'qish · Reading Part 4 format

Learning Without Walls

When schools around the world closed in 2020, millions of students suddenly found themselves learning through a screen. For some it was a disaster; for others it was the beginning of a new way of studying. Five years later, online education is no longer an emergency solution. It has become a normal part of life for students of all ages.

The advantages are obvious. A student in a small village can now watch lectures from the best universities in the world, often for free. Online courses are flexible: learners can study at night, pause a video, and watch difficult parts again. For adults with jobs and families, this flexibility is often the only way to continue their education.

However, research shows that online learning is not equally effective for everyone. A study of 50,000 university students found that weaker students did worse online than in the classroom, while strong students did equally well in both. The main problem, researchers say, is self-discipline. At home there are many distractions — phones, games, family — and without a teacher in the room, it is easy to fall behind.

Another problem is the lack of interaction. Learning is not only about information; it is also about discussion, questions and friendship. Many students say they felt lonely during online courses, and some lost their motivation completely. "I could watch a lecture," one student remembers, "but I couldn't turn to my friend and say, 'Did you understand that?'"

Because of this, many schools now use a mix of both methods, known as blended learning. Students learn new information online at their own speed and then come to class to practise, discuss and ask questions. Early results are promising: in schools using blended learning, students' marks have risen by an average of eight per cent.

Experts believe that the future of education will not be online or offline, but both. As long as students learn how to manage their time and teachers learn how to use technology wisely, the classroom of the future may have no walls at all.

1.What happened to online education after 2020?
2.Who benefits most from flexible courses?
3.What did the study of 50,000 students show?
4.What is blended learning?
5.Online lectures from top universities are sometimes free.
6.Most students prefer online learning to classes.
7.Some students felt lonely during online courses.
8.Marks in blended-learning schools have gone down.
9.The writer thinks online learning will replace schools.

Learning from the Village

Aziza lives in a small village in Surkhandarya, three hundred kilometres from the nearest university. Two years ago she started a distance learning degree in computer science. 'If I hadn't had this chance, I would be working in the fields now,' she says. Every morning she watches video lessons and then does her assignments. The biggest problem is the connection: when it rains, the internet often stops working. Aziza admits that self-discipline is not easy. 'At home there are a lot of distractions — my little brothers, housework, my phone.' However, she has never missed a deadline. Her dream is to work remotely for an international company.

2.She is studying computer science.
3.The internet sometimes stops when it rains.
